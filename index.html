02 / Background

Engineering roots,
finance conviction.

A B.Tech in Computer Science gave me the engineering instincts; an MSc in Quantitative Finance at Manchester gave me the pricing, risk and econometrics behind them. In between: an investment banking internship structuring credit proposals, a year at EY India in technology & finance consulting, and a dissertation that tested 319 firm characteristics to find the ones that actually predict returns. That combination is why the coverage above spans IB memos and PySpark pipelines with the same rigour.

Why this work. Testing 319 firm characteristics and keeping 15 taught me to distrust anything that looks predictive until it's been stress-tested — the same instinct that runs through every project here, whether I'm underwriting a borrower, pricing a debt instrument, or shipping a data pipeline. I'm drawn to problems where a wrong assumption costs real money and the fix is a cleaner model, not a longer report.

Where I'm headed. Toward roles that sit at the point where capital gets allocated and risk gets priced — investment banking, credit and market risk, and quantitative research — built on the same data and engineering discipline that runs through this whole portfolio. I want to be the person a desk trusts to build the model and know exactly where it breaks.
