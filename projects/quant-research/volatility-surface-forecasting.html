Forecasting the volatility surface

Deep learning models tested the way a derivatives desk actually cares about: does it make money after hedging and costs

Most implied volatility research stops at forecast accuracy. This project pushes further, training an LSTM with attention and a ConvLSTM on 26 years of daily SPX options data to forecast the full implied volatility surface, then wiring those forecasts directly into a delta-hedged short strangle strategy with real transaction costs, to test whether a more accurate surface forecast actually translates into a better trading outcome.

The build

ModelsLSTM with additive attention, and ConvLSTM treating the surface as a spatio-temporal grid
Loss functionMAE + RMSE + QLIKE, with a no-arbitrage penalty term
Trading strategy30-day, ±10Δ short strangle, daily delta-hedged, VIX-based entry gate, stop-loss
Backtest window2004 to 2022, common across all three strategies compared
02 / Why Two Architectures

Sequence memory versus spatial coherence

The LSTM with attention learns to weight historically informative time windows, which makes it strong at picking up regime shifts, a sudden change in how the market is pricing volatility. The ConvLSTM instead treats the volatility surface as an evolving spatio-temporal grid, convolving across neighbouring deltas and maturities to preserve cross-maturity coherence, so a forecast for the 30-day tenor stays consistent with the forecast for the 60-day tenor next to it, rather than each maturity being predicted independently and potentially producing an internally inconsistent, arbitrageable surface.

That structural consistency turns out to matter more than pointwise accuracy. The project's own framing is direct about this: it finds that the model with better structural consistency, the ConvLSTM, outperforms in trading even when it is not always the single most accurate model on a point-by-point forecasting metric.

03 / Results

Forecast quality translated into a trading outcome

Backtested strangle strategy, 2004 to 2022
StrategyAnnualised returnSharpe ratio
ConvLSTM-driven hedging31.3%1.37
Rule-based, realised IV hedging24.0%0.96
LSTM-Attention-driven hedging22.2%0.89

The ConvLSTM-driven strategy also showed the strongest resilience during high-VIX regimes, the exact conditions where a poorly hedged short-volatility book tends to lose the most. Evaluation went beyond raw Sharpe and return: MAE, RMSE, QLIKE, vega-weighted error, directional hit rate, and surface-shape diagnostics like skew and term-structure integrity were all checked, alongside attention-map interpretability to understand which historical windows the LSTM was actually weighting.

04 / Robustness

Not a single lucky backtest window

Results are validated with a walk-forward split rather than a single train-test divide, and stress-tested with stationary bootstrap resampling and sensitivity analysis across the stop-loss threshold, the VIX entry gate, hedge smoothing, and cost assumptions. Running the same comparison across many resampled paths and parameter choices, rather than reporting the single best-looking configuration, is what separates a strategy that is genuinely robust from one that was tuned to a specific backtest window.

05 / What This Demonstrates

Machine learning judged by P&L, not just loss curves

A forecasting model that never gets tested against a real trading outcome, with real hedging frictions and transaction costs, is an academic exercise. Wiring the ConvLSTM's surface forecasts directly into a delta-hedged strategy, comparing it honestly against a simpler rule-based benchmark, and finding that structural consistency beats pointwise accuracy is the kind of result that would actually inform a derivatives desk's model choice, not just a machine learning leaderboard.
