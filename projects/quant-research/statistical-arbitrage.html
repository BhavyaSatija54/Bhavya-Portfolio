Statistical Arbitrage

128 equities, 20 years, 76 walk-forward windows, and a Sharpe ratio reported two ways

A cointegration-based pairs trading pipeline on 128 large and mid-cap US equities from 1997 to 2017, using Johansen tests, Ornstein-Uhlenbeck spread modelling, and Kalman-filtered hedge ratios. The headline Sharpe is 0.68 net of costs. The write-up is upfront that a survivorship-bias correction likely brings that closer to 0.34 to 0.47, and treats 0.68 as a ceiling, not the answer.

02 / Methodology

Screening, calibration, and execution, in that order

Pair screening runs a Johansen trace test on log-prices to identify candidate pairs, confirmed by an ADF test on the resulting spread and filtered to a half-life between 8 and 45 trading days, so pairs that revert too fast to be a real relationship or too slowly to be tradeable net of costs get dropped. Each surviving spread is fit as an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process via OLS on its discretised AR(1) form, giving the mean-reversion speed and stationary standard deviation used for z-scoring, rather than a rolling sample standard deviation that would add a second uncontrolled parameter.

Execution is where most backtests quietly cheat. This one enters at a z-score above 2.5, exits under 0.75, stops out above 3.5, and implements a signal decided on one day's close at the next day's open, so there is no same-bar execution. Costs run roughly 5 basis points per leg, scaled by a square-root market-impact term, and a 3% average-daily-volume participation cap limits how much of a desired position can actually fill on a given day, with both profit and cost computed from the realised fill rather than the target size.

Two crisis or high-dispersion regimes, covering only 55% of the trading days in the sample, produced 80% of the strategy's total compounded return, at roughly double the Sharpe of the calmer periods in between. That pattern matches the "crowded, low-dispersion regime compresses stat-arb returns" finding documented in the Khandani and Lo statistical arbitrage literature, which is a useful sanity check that this result is not an artefact of this specific dataset.

Testing improvements honestly, including the ones that fail

The Kalman-filtered dynamic hedge, which sounds like a strict improvement over a static hedge ratio, actually underperforms because a beta that updates daily also reacts to daily noise, whipsawing entries around short-lived estimation error. Three-asset baskets underperform because a third leg adds idiosyncratic risk without a proportional gain in mean-reversion strength. Reporting both of these as negative results, rather than quietly dropping them from the write-up, is exactly what separates a research process from a result that was reverse-engineered to look good.

05 / Honest Limitations

Why 0.68 is a ceiling, not the answer

Survivorship biasThe universe only includes securities that traded continuously for the full 20 years, so anything delisted through bankruptcy or acquisition is structurally excluded. Prior pairs-trading replication studies suggest Sharpe ratios typically drop 30 to 50% once delisted securities are included, which puts a bias-corrected estimate around 0.34 to 0.47.

Statistical significance testing backs the core result even after accounting for that caveat. A block-bootstrap on the Sharpe ratio, 5,000 resamples with 21-day blocks, gives a 95% confidence interval of 0.35 to 1.01, and a binomial test on the window-level win rate, 47 of 71 windows positive, rejects a 50% random hit rate with a p-value of 0.004. A factor regression against market, sector, and volatility proxies explains only 2.5% of return variance, meaning the strategy is overwhelmingly idiosyncratic rather than a disguised market or sector bet.

06 / What This Demonstrates

Research discipline, not just a backtest

Any strategy can post a good Sharpe ratio if the backtest is generous enough. This project is built the other way around: realistic costs, a participation cap that constrains fills as size grows, a capacity frontier that shows the edge decaying honestly rather than staying flat, and three extensions reported with their actual results, including the two that made things worse. That is the standard a quant research desk actually holds a strategy to before it gets capital.
