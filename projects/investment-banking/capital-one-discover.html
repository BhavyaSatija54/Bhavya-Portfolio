02 / Situation Overview

Why this deal, and why it took 14 months

Discover brought Capital One something no issuer had owned since American Express spun off its own network decades earlier: a proprietary, four party payment network sitting underneath a card book. For an issuer of Capital One's scale, routing volume onto Discover Network rather than paying Visa or Mastercard's swipe fees is a direct margin lever, and that is where most of the $1.2bn network synergy target comes from. The other half of the case, roughly $1.5bn of cost synergies, follows from combining two card centric operating platforms.

The review took materially longer than a typical regional bank deal because this was the largest bank merger since the 2008 financial crisis, it carried an explicit payment network dimension the Department of Justice needed to assess separately from the banking regulators, and it landed in a period of heightened political scrutiny of large bank consolidation, including a public 147 witness OCC hearing. Discover also walked into the deal with a self identified card tier misclassification issue and a new CEO appointed in late 2023, both of which were priced into the 26.6% premium rather than treated as separate deal breakers. The Federal Reserve and OCC granted conditional approval on April 18, 2025, and the transaction closed a month later.

04 / Accretion & Dilution

Adjusted versus GAAP, and why they disagree in 2025

The deal is accretive on an adjusted, non GAAP basis in every projected year, rising from roughly 6% in 2025 to above 18% by 2028. The GAAP picture tells a different story in the first year: a one time, day one CECL allowance build of $8.8bn on the acquired non PCD loan book, recognised entirely through 2025 provision expense, makes the deal sharply GAAP dilutive in year one before flipping solidly accretive from 2026 onward. Both pictures are true at once, and a candidate who can only quote the headline accretion number without explaining the CECL mechanics has not really underwritten the deal.

Management's own guidance, double digit adjusted accretion in 2026 and above 15% in 2027, sits directionally in line with this build, which is itself a useful check on the model's assumptions.

06 / Sensitivity

What moves the accretion number

The model recomputes the exchange ratio, shares issued and synergy dollars for every scenario rather than scaling a single base case. Holding synergy realisation at 100%, 2027 adjusted accretion ranges from roughly 23% at a hypothetical 15% premium down to about 18% at the actual 26.6% premium paid, since a higher premium means more shares issued and more dilution to absorb. Holding the actual premium fixed, accretion falls from 18% at full synergy realisation to roughly 8% if only half the guided synergies show up, which is the single most important execution risk in the whole deal.

Why the model was built this wayThe illustrative bridge in Part A deliberately uses the $35.3bn announced value rather than the $51.8bn actual consideration, since the actual purchase accounting reflects COF's share price at closing rather than at announcement, information not available at signing. 08 / Regulatory & Credit Concentration Risk

A scored risk register, not a checklist

Seven regulatory and reputational risks and two credit concentration risks were scored on a five point likelihood and impact scale at signing. Every item below also records what actually happened, since the deal has now closed and most of the regulatory risk has resolved.

Card & unsecured consumer credit concentration

Score 16, highest

Pro forma combined card receivables exceed $250bn, roughly $150bn of COF domestic card plus $102bn of DFS card, concentrating earnings in a single, cyclical asset class rather than diversifying it.

Ongoing, inherent to the deal thesis
Mitigant Diversification from Discover's direct to consumer deposit franchise and student loan run off book, plus combined scale that supports through the cycle loss absorption capacity.

Federal Reserve and OCC systemic review

Score 15

Bank Merger Act review including a public OCC hearing, given the deal's size and consumer lending concentration.

Resolved, conditional approval granted April 18, 2025 after a roughly 14 month review including a 147 witness hearing
Mitigant A roughly 14% pro forma CET1 ratio and a $265bn, five year community benefits plan directly addressed the safety and soundness and community impact concerns raised.

Subprime and near prime borrower sensitivity

Score 12

Both franchises skew toward revolving card balances sensitive to unemployment and wage growth, and both companies' net charge offs rose sharply in 2023.

Ongoing, increases combined earnings volatility through a credit cycle
Mitigant The $8.8bn day one non PCD CECL allowance build materially front loads reserve coverage on the acquired book, reducing forward loss recognition volatility relative to an unreserved acquisition.

Antitrust and market concentration

Score 12

DOJ Antitrust Division review of combined share in general purpose credit card issuance and payment network competition, given the deal adds a network to an issuer.

Resolved, the DOJ did not formally challenge the merger, despite public opposition from some lawmakers
Mitigant COF and DFS card portfolios are largely complementary by credit tier, and Discover Network remains a distant fourth payment network, limiting horizontal overlap concerns.

Discover's pre-existing compliance issues

Score 9

Discover had self identified a card tier misclassification issue affecting merchant interchange, and faced heightened compliance scrutiny alongside a new CEO appointment in December 2023, shortly before the deal.

Mitigated at diligence, priced into the 26.6% premium
Mitigant COF's public commitment to extend its compliance and risk infrastructure to Discover is an explicit closing condition and post close workstream.

Payment network execution risk

Score 9

The strategy depends on migrating a portion of COF's debit and credit volume onto the smaller Discover Network rather than Visa or Mastercard, which carries materially less global merchant acceptance.

Ongoing, the single largest execution dependent line in the synergy case
Mitigant Phased volume migration, targeting roughly $175bn of purchase volume onto Discover Network by 2027, rather than a one time cutover.

Integration and execution risk

Score 9

Large scale technology, operations and cultural integration of two card centric but organisationally distinct institutions.

Ongoing, integration costs already guided to modestly exceed the original $2.8bn estimate
Mitigant COF's own issuer and network experience, combined with a phased 2025 to 2027 cost synergy ramp, reduces though does not eliminate big bang integration risk.

CRA and community reinvestment commitments

Score 8

Regulatory and public pressure to commit to community lending, investment and philanthropy as a condition of approval.

Resolved, a $265bn, five year community benefits plan was committed in July 2024
Mitigant The plan was pre-negotiated with community groups ahead of final approval, and its costs are modest relative to deal scale when phased over five years.

Consumer protection and pre-existing litigation

Score 6

Shareholder and consumer class actions, including a paused antitrust suit and a legacy COF savings rate mis-selling matter.

Partially resolved, COF agreed a $425mm settlement on the 360 Performance Savings claims shortly before closing
Mitigant The settlement removed a key closing conditions overhang, and residual litigation risk is manageable relative to deal size and is a COF legacy exposure rather than one created by the deal.

Political and reputational risk

Score 6

Election cycle scrutiny of large bank consolidation, with vocal opposition from certain lawmakers, including Senator Warren, and advocacy groups.

Resolved at approval, the deal closed with full regulatory sign off notwithstanding public opposition
Mitigant Extensive, proactive community benefits and compliance commitments reduced the political leverage available against approval.
TakeawayThis combination is unusual among large bank mergers in that it concentrates, rather than diversifies, credit risk, since both franchises are overwhelmingly unsecured consumer and card lenders. Regulators ultimately judged that manageable given the all stock structure, an unusually large day one reserve build, and extensive community benefit commitments, but the combined entity now carries meaningfully more single asset class concentration than either standalone institution, and that is something to monitor through the credit cycle rather than treat as a closed diligence item.

10 / Methodology & What This Demonstrates

How the model is built, and why that matters

Independently constructed, fully formula driven analyst model

Deal value, exchange ratio, premium, synergy guidance, integration cost guidance, the day one CECL allowance build, and the actual pro forma CET1 outcome are drawn directly from company filings, press releases and earnings calls. Multi year projections, the organic growth rate applied to standalone earnings, and certain purchase accounting line items such as intangible amortisation phasing are illustrative analyst estimates, calibrated to land on management's disclosed outcomes and clearly labelled as such throughout the workbook rather than presented as fact.

This is the standard the rest of the work on this site is held to: build the base case, show where the assumption is a judgement call rather than a disclosed fact, and check the output against what the company actually reported once it becomes available. The model reconciles its own illustrative CET1 bridge to within 0.05 percentage points of the actual disclosed ratio at close, which is the kind of check that matters more than any single headline number.
