02 / Situation Overview

From growth at all costs to a disciplined listing

Klarna's valuation history is a case study in how quickly a growth multiple can compress. Revenue grew every single year from 2021 through 2025, from $1.85bn to $3.51bn, yet the company's valuation fell 85% between its 2021 peak and its 2022 trough. That was never really a revenue story. As 2021's growth at all costs environment gave way to rising rates and tighter BNPL regulatory scrutiny in 2022, investors simply stopped paying a euphoric multiple for the same growing revenue base, and the re-rating bridge below shows exactly how much of each move was fundamentals versus sentiment.

By the time Klarna actually listed in September 2025, the company had also crossed into disciplined profitability, reporting its first Adjusted Operating Profit in FY2024 of $181mm before a smaller reported figure of $65mm in FY2025, alongside continued GMV and revenue growth. That combination, proven growth plus a credible profitability inflection, is what let the IPO price at $40 a share rather than at either historical extreme, and the order book was reportedly oversubscribed roughly 26 times, pushing the stock to a $45.82 close on day one.

05 / DCF Valuation

Why the DCF sits below the offer price, on purpose

Explicit methodology caveat, documented in the model itself

A standard unlevered free cash flow DCF is a simplified fit for a regulated, credit exposed digital bank, where deposits and wholesale funding are the operating capital structure rather than a financing afterthought. The model is upfront about this rather than presenting the DCF as a definitive answer, and layers in an explicit regulatory capital drag charge as the loan book grows, since Klarna Bank AB cannot fund all of its loan growth with deposits and debt alone under Basel III.

PayPal and Block are large, diversified and only partially BNPL exposed, and Adyen is a global acquiring platform rather than a direct comparable, so all three pull the broad average down without being decision relevant. Affirm, the closest pure play BNPL peer, trades at 10.0x, and applying a 3.0x to 5.5x range to Klarna's FY2026E revenue of $4.4bn, a discount to Affirm reflecting Klarna's thinner margins and heavier consumer credit exposure at listing, implies an equity value of roughly $14.4bn to $25.4bn. That range comfortably brackets both the $15.1bn offer and the $19.65bn day-1 close, which is the strongest evidence in this whole analysis that the IPO was priced with reference to public trading multiples rather than to a pure cash flow view.

The DCF and the comps tell different stories, and that gap is itself the finding. The DCF sits below the actual price because most of its value comes from a terminal assumption that is a simplification for a regulated bank. The comps bracket the actual price comfortably, because the market was pricing Klarna against Affirm's public multiple, adjusted down for Klarna's thinner margins, rather than against a discounted cash flow view. Neither the 2021 peak nor the 2022 trough falls inside any fundamentals based range in this analysis, reinforcing that both were sentiment and multiple driven rather than changes in the underlying business.

09 / Methodology & What This Demonstrates

Sourced where possible, flagged where estimated

FY2023A through FY2025A income statement figures, including provision for credit losses, processing and servicing costs, funding costs and every operating expense line, are exact figures from Klarna's filed 6-K consolidated statements, not model estimates. FY2023A opening balance sheet items and FY2026E onward projections are calibrated, formula driven estimates, clearly labelled as such and cross-checked against Klarna's actual June 2025 disclosed balance sheet data where available.

The point of building both a DCF and a comparable company set, rather than picking whichever one flatters a target price, is that disagreement between methodologies is informative. Here it shows that Klarna was priced as a growth fintech trading on revenue multiples, not as a bank trading on a cash flow view, which is exactly the kind of judgement call an equity research or ECM analyst needs to make explicit rather than bury inside a single blended number.
